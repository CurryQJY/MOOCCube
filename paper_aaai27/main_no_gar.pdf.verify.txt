Course-Knowledge-Guided Reinforcement Learning for Strict Course-Cold
MOOC Recommendation
Anonymous submission

Abstract
MOOC platforms must rank newly released courses before
those courses collect interactions, but standard offline protocols can make this setting appear easier than deployed
full-catalog ranking. Sampled candidates remove many warm
competitors, and interaction-weighted averages can be driven
by a few frequently requested cold courses. We study strict
course-cold MOOC recommendation, where target courses
have zero training interactions, compete against the full catalog, and are evaluated by course-macro Recall/NDCG. The
benchmark asks whether each zero-interaction course, rather
than only each cold-course interaction, receives early exposure while remaining pedagogically plausible. We propose Course-Knowledge-Guided Reinforcement Learning
(CKG-RL), which anchors course representations in content,
masks cold-course ID evidence, refines course states through
retrieval-based learner simulation, and uses concept, prerequisite, difficulty, and redundancy signals in sampling and rewards. On MOOCCube, Junyi, and COCO, CKG-RL has the
highest mean in every @5/@10 column, with relative gains
from +3.5% to +33.1%; Holm-corrected paired tests support
11 of 12 early-rank comparisons. MOOCCube ablations attribute the gains to educational rewards, knowledge-guided
sampling, and prerequisite supervision. These results suggest
that strict course-cold ranking benefits from making educational constraints part of candidate construction and reward
design, rather than treating course knowledge only as side
features.

Introduction
MOOC platforms continually add courses, course versions,
and learning paths. Recommenders therefore must rank
courses before interaction histories are available, a persistent cold-start condition (Schein et al. 2002; Gantner et al.
2010; Lin et al. 2024; Zhang et al. 2025). In education,
cold-start ranking is not only a sparsity problem. A useful course should be topically relevant, prerequisite-safe,
difficulty-appropriate, and non-redundant with prior learning. Public MOOC resources expose such course-side signals
through metadata, concepts, and prerequisite relations (Yu
et al. 2020, 2021).

Common offline protocols can hide this risk. Sampled
candidate sets may remove the warm courses that dominate the real catalog (Krichene and Rendle 2020), and reproducibility studies show that weak protocols and baselines
can overstate progress (Dacrema, Cremonesi, and Jannach
2019). Interaction-weighted averages may also be driven by a
few high-volume cold courses, echoing recent concerns that
mean ranking utility can conceal tail-risk failures (Takehi,
Diaz, and Sakai 2026).
We study strict course-cold MOOC recommendation: target courses have zero training interactions, compete against
the full catalog, and are evaluated by course-macro Recall/NDCG. The evaluation unit is the cold course itself rather
than a cold-course interaction drawn from a popular course.
This benchmark forces the model to answer two questions at
once: whether a new course can obtain early exposure at all,
and whether the exposed course is educationally plausible
for the learner.
Figure 1 makes this motivation concrete on MOOCCube.
The closest content-graph baseline, CGRC, still leaves 46%
of seed-course cases with NDCG@10 no greater than 0.10
under full-catalog course-cold ranking. The same cold-test
setting also exhibits large prerequisite and difficulty gaps and
sparse concept continuity, indicating that cold MOOC recommendation must address both exposure and pedagogical
fit. These diagnostics motivate placing course knowledge inside retrieval, sampling, and reward design rather than treating it as a passive course feature.
CKG-RL uses course knowledge in four places: content
anchoring, cold-course ID masking, retrieval-based learner
simulation, and rewards over concepts, prerequisites, difficulty, and redundancy. Educational knowledge therefore
affects the ranking dynamics instead of appearing only as
appended course features.
Across MOOCCube, Junyi, and COCO, CKG-RL has
higher @10 course-macro means than the strongest nonCKG baseline under strict course-cold full-ranking evaluation. We restrict this conclusion to early-rank exposure for
zero-interaction courses. Some baselines remain competitive
on broader or hot-course diagnostics.

Offline recommender evaluation is sensitive to metric
choice, candidate construction, and reproducibility practice (Sun et al. 2020; Hidasi and Czapp 2023; Bauer,
Zangerle, and Said 2024). Sampled negatives can change
comparisons relative to full ranking (Krichene and Rendle 2020; Li et al. 2024), while distribution-aware evaluation exposes variation across poorly served groups or
courses (Ekstrand, Carterette, and Diaz 2024). We apply
this concern to course-cold recommendation: the unit at risk
is a held-out course, and the failure is no useful exposure
under full-catalog ranking (Takehi, Diaz, and Sakai 2026).
Reinforcement-learning recommenders model sequential decisions (Shani, Heckerman, and Brafman 2005; Zhao et al.
2018; Chen et al. 2023). CKG-RL uses a narrower simulator for learner-course matching under course-cold evidence
constraints.

Preliminaries
Let U denote the set of learners and C denote the set of
courses. The static training interactions satisfy Dtrain ⊆
U × C; an observed pair (u, c) records that learner u ∈ U
interacted with course c ∈ C. Given Dtrain and course-side
information, a recommender produces, for each learner u, a
ranked list Πu over candidate courses.
Each course c has a content vector xc and educational
signals. We use concept overlap, prerequisite links, training popularity, and history similarity as proxies for topical continuity, readiness, difficulty, and redundancy, consistent with pedagogical-fit views of educational recommendation (da Silva et al. 2023; Rahayu, Ferdiana, and Kusumawardani 2023). These signals are available even when c has
no training interactions. They are not complete learner-state
measurements.

Main Objective
The objective is to learn a logit ℓ(u, c) that ranks c+ ∈
Ccold highly within Au , using learner history and courseside evidence but no interaction-derived target-course signal.
Hot-course metrics are diagnostics for the trade-off between
zero-interaction generalization and warm-course ranking.

Method
Overview
CKG-RL makes the evidence boundary of strict coursecold ranking explicit. For each learner-course pair (u, c),
the model builds a learner representation zu from the learner
history and an initial course embedding ec from course-side
evidence. A simulator starts from ec , refines it through T
learner-matching steps, and returns the final course embedding zc,T := hT,c . The ranking score then matches zc,T with
the target learner representation.
Three design constraints connect the architecture to the
two-part motivation. Content anchoring and forced masking
enforce the zero-interaction evidence boundary. Simulation
exposes cold representations to plausible learner states under full-catalog competition. Course knowledge then filters
matches that are semantically related but prerequisite-unsafe,
too difficult, or redundant. Figure 2 summarizes the training
and evaluation flow.

Content-Anchored Course Representation
The content branch provides the evidence available for a
course with zero training interactions, a role emphasized in
content-based and LLM-assisted recommendation (Liu et al.
2024a; Wu et al. 2024). Each learner u has a trainable ID
embedding pu , projected into the recommendation space:
zu = fu (pu ).

(7)

Each course c has a trainable ID embedding vc and a frozen
768-dimensional content embedding xc . A content projection network maps xc into the same latent space:
cc = fc (xc ).

(8)

The initial course embedding is a gated fusion of the projected content vector and a possibly masked ID vector:
gc = σ (Wg [v̄c ; cc ]) ,

ec = gc ⊙ v̄c + (1 − gc ) ⊙ cc . (9)

Here fu and fc are learned projection networks, Wg is a
learned gate matrix, σ is the sigmoid function, and ⊙ denotes elementwise multiplication. v̄c is the ID branch after
masking and ID dropout. The gate lets warm courses use collaborative evidence when available, while the content branch
defines ec when ID evidence is unavailable.

Implementation Details
The implementation uses 128-dimensional learner and
course embeddings, a 256-dimensional hidden layer, and
768-dimensional course content features. The MOOCCube/MOOCCubeX and COCO content features are BERT
[CLS] representations (Devlin et al. 2019), using Chinese and English encoders respectively; Junyi is treated
as an educational course generalization setting with processed course-content features. The simulator uses horizon
T = 5 with update rate η = 0.3. At each step, it retrieves M = 256 users, samples N = 20 candidates with
τs = 0.20 and ϵ = 0.10, and reorders candidates with
knowledge-sampling weight β = 0.20. The educational reward weights are 0.08 for prerequisite penalty, 0.04 for concept bonus, 0.03 for difficulty penalty, and 0.02 for redundancy penalty. We use nsat = 5, δpre = 0.20, µC = 0.12,
µR = 0.70, and γR = 1.0. The target-anchor coefficients are
αcold = 0.35, αhot = 0.60, αstep = 0.20, αent = 0.20,
and [αmin , αmax ] = [0.15, 0.85]. PPO uses two epochs,
γ = 0.90, λ = 0.95, clipping radius ϵp = 0.20, value
clipping 0.20, and advantage normalization. The final strict
course-cold runs train for 60 epochs with batch size 2048
and select checkpoints using cold course-macro validation
behavior. The reported configuration uses only the components described in this section.

Experiments
Dataset and Protocol
The experiments use three educational recommendation settings, summarized in Table 1. MOOCCube is the courseknowledge analysis setting: it provides learner-course interactions, and MOOCCubeX supplies course-concept and prerequisite evidence (Yu et al. 2020, 2021). Junyi is included
as a third educational course recommendation setting under
the same strict course-cold evaluator (Chen, Hsieh, and Tsai
2020), but it is not used for the course-relation ablations.

COCO is the large-catalog main-table setting and provides a
public semantic collection of online courses with a preprocessed knowledge graph over course metadata (Dessì et al.
2018).
The strict course-cold balanced protocol uses three random seeds, 2025, 2026, and 2027. Warm interactions are
split 80/10/10 before cold-course reinsertion. Eligible cold
courses are assigned to 20 popularity-balanced folds: one
validation fold and two test folds, giving validation/test cold
course ratios of 0.05/0.10. All interactions of validation and
test cold courses are removed from training, while test users
must retain training history, isolating course rather than user
cold-start. The final course-macro evaluation contains about
68 MOOCCube cold courses, 71 Junyi cold courses, and 820
COCO cold courses per seed. After train-history masking,
COCO uses about 6,048 warm candidate courses per seed.
The split is a controlled course-cold benchmark, not a
chronological launch-time study. Primary metrics are fullranking course-macro Recall@K and NDCG@K, K ∈
{5, 10, 20}. Hot course-macro metrics are auxiliary diagnostics. This separates zero-interaction generalization, fullcatalog competition, and whether gains cover cold courses
rather than concentrating on high-volume ones (Bauer,
Zangerle, and Said 2024; Ekstrand, Carterette, and Diaz
2024).

Research Questions
The experiments answer four questions. RQ1: What strict
course-cold full-ranking course-macro scores does CKG-RL
obtain relative to the baselines? RQ2: Which exposure and
pedagogical-risk patterns motivate the method components?
RQ3: Which course-knowledge components contribute under the same protocol? RQ4: How stable are the gains under
design variations, and what cost does CKG-RL introduce?

Baselines
To keep the comparison protocol-controlled, all reported
cells use the same course-cold splits, full-catalog candidate set, train-history masking, course-macro evaluator, and
validation cold NDCG@10 checkpoint rule (Sun et al.
2020; Hidasi and Czapp 2023). We compare against several capability classes. Interaction-only baselines include
Popularity, BPR (Rendle et al. 2009), and LightGCN (He
et al. 2020). They test whether learner-side history or
graph propagation can rank target courses whose own
training edges are removed. Side-feature fallback uses
DropoutNet (Volkovs, Yu, and Poutanen 2017). Contenttransfer uses CCFCRec (Zhou, Zhang, and Yang 2023),
ALDI (Huang, Wang, and Huang 2023), and SEMCo (Meehan and Pauwels 2026). Knowledge-graph and graphreconstruction baselines include KGRec (Yang et al.
2023), CGRC (Kim et al. 2024), and the adapted courserecommendation model PCGNN (Sun et al. 2024); CGRC is
the closest baseline because it reconstructs cold-course structure from course content. DropoutNet, ALDI, and SEMCo
retain their released training objectives while using the common data interface and final evaluator. KGRec follows its
original formulation and objective. CCFCRec uses a public-

source static-HIN wrapper; and CGRC is reimplemented
from the paper because no official code was available.

Theoretical Complexity
Let E be the number of training interactions, I the catalog
size, Q the number of evaluation cases, and d the embedding dimension. CKG-RL adds a bounded simulator term
O(ET (M d + N d + H)) during training, where T = 5,
M = 256, and N = 20 in the reported runs. After course representations are cached, full-ranking inference uses the same
dominant dot-product term O(QId) as embedding baselines.

Discussion and Limitations
The experiments point to the evaluation unit as a source
of hidden risk. Interaction-weighted metrics can improve
when a model serves a few high-volume cold courses, even if
many other cold courses receive no useful rank; distributionaware evaluation is designed to expose this pattern (Ekstrand,
Carterette, and Diaz 2024; Bauer, Zangerle, and Said 2024).

Conclusion
We studied strict course-cold MOOC recommendation,
where target courses have no training interactions and fullranking course-macro evaluation exposes failures hidden by
interaction averages. CKG-RL combines content anchoring,
forced-cold masking, retrieval-based simulation, and courseknowledge rewards. Across MOOCCube, Junyi, and COCO,
it yields higher mean early-rank course-cold metrics under
the same full-ranking course-macro protocol. The results indicate that reducing course-cold exposure risk requires both
content transfer and educational constraints.

Ethical Statement
Experiments use public MOOCCube/MOOCCubeX, Junyi,
and COCO data plus derived course representations. Released artifacts should exclude personally identifiable information and follow dataset terms. Deployment requires online validation and monitoring for visibility, fairness, transparency, and consequential use (Stray et al. 2024; Zhao et al.
2025).
