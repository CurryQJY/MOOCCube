Course-Knowledge-Guided Reinforcement Learning for Strict Item-Cold MOOC
Recommendation
Anonymous submission

Abstract
MOOC platforms need to rank newly released courses before those courses collect interactions. Standard offline protocols can make this setting appear easier than it is: sampled
candidates remove many warm competitors, and interactionweighted averages can be driven by a few frequently requested
cold courses. We study strict item-cold MOOC recommendation, where target courses have zero training interactions,
compete against the full catalog, and are evaluated by itemmacro Recall/NDCG. The benchmark asks whether each zerointeraction course, not only each cold-course interaction, receives early exposure while remaining pedagogically plausible. We propose Course-Knowledge-Guided Reinforcement
Learning (CKG-RL), which anchors course representations in
content, masks cold-course ID evidence, refines course states
through retrieval-based learner simulation, and uses concept,
prerequisite, difficulty, and redundancy signals in sampling
and rewards. On MOOCCube, Junyi, and COCO, CKG-RL
improves the strongest non-CKG baseline at @10 under strict
item-cold item-macro evaluation. MOOCCube ablations attribute the gains to educational rewards, knowledge-guided
sampling, and prerequisite supervision. These findings suggest that, under full-catalog item-macro evaluation, content
transfer is not enough; educational constraints must affect the
ranking dynamics.

Introduction
MOOC platforms continually add courses, course versions,
and learning paths. Recommenders therefore must rank
courses before interaction histories are available, a persistent
cold-start condition (????). In education, cold-start ranking
is not only a sparsity problem. A useful course should be topically relevant, prerequisite-safe, difficulty-appropriate, and
non-redundant with prior learning. Public MOOC resources
expose such course-side signals through metadata, concepts,
and prerequisite relations (??).
Common offline protocols can hide this risk. Sampled
candidate sets may remove the warm courses that dominate the real catalog (?), and reproducibility studies show
that weak protocols and baselines can overstate progress (?).

• We define a strict item-cold MOOC benchmark with fullcatalog item-macro Recall/NDCG as the primary target.
• We introduce CKG-RL, which combines content anchoring, forced-cold masking, retrieval-based simulation, and

• We evaluate three datasets against collaborative, graph,

Method
Overview
CKG-RL makes the evidence boundary of strict item-cold
ranking explicit. For each learner-course pair (u, c), the
model builds a learner representation zu from the learner
history and an initial course embedding ec from course-side
evidence. A simulator starts from ec , refines it through T
learner-matching steps, and returns the final course embedding zc,T := hT,c . The ranking score then matches zc,T with
the target learner representation.
Three design constraints connect the architecture to the
two-part motivation. Content anchoring and forced masking
enforce the zero-interaction evidence boundary. Simulation
exposes cold representations to plausible learner states under full-catalog competition. Course knowledge then filters
matches that are semantically related but prerequisite-unsafe,
too difficult, or redundant. Figure ?? summarizes the training
and evaluation flow.

Forced-Cold ID Masking
Forced-cold masking enforces the protocol inside the model.
Let pc be the training-set popularity of course c. Under the
strict item-cold protocol, a course is cold when pc < 1,
which is equivalent to having zero training interactions. For
cold examples, CKG-RL removes the course ID evidence:

This keeps the representation used at inference aligned with

Implementation Details
The implementation uses 128-dimensional learner and
course embeddings, a 256-dimensional hidden layer, and
768-dimensional course content features. The MOOCCube/MOOCCubeX and COCO content features are BERT
[CLS] representations (?), using Chinese and English encoders respectively; Junyi is treated as an educational item
generalization setting with processed item-content features.
The simulator uses horizon T = 5 with update rate η = 0.3.
At each step, it retrieves M = 256 users, samples N = 20
candidates with τs = 0.20 and ϵ = 0.10, and reorders candidates with knowledge-sampling weight β = 0.20. The educational reward weights are 0.08 for prerequisite penalty,
0.04 for concept bonus, 0.03 for difficulty penalty, and 0.02
for redundancy penalty. The target-anchor coefficients are
αcold = 0.35, αhot = 0.60, αstep = 0.20, αent = 0.20,
and [αmin , αmax ] = [0.15, 0.85]. PPO uses two epochs,
γ = 0.90, λ = 0.95, clip ratio 0.2, value clipping 0.20,
and advantage normalization. The final strict item-cold runs
train for 60 epochs with batch size 2048 and select checkpoints using cold item-macro validation behavior. The reported configuration uses only the components described in
this section.

Baselines
To keep the comparison protocol-controlled, all baselines
use the same item-cold splits, full-catalog candidate set,

against four capability classes. Interaction-only baselines
include Popularity, BPR (?), and LightGCN (?). They test
whether learner-side history or graph propagation can rank
target courses whose own training edges are removed. Sidefeature fallback uses DropoutNet (?). Content-transfer
uses CCFCRec (?), ALDI (?), and SEMCo (?). Graph
reconstruction uses CGRC (?), the closest baseline because it reconstructs cold-item structure from item content.

and CGRC is a paper-faithful reimplementation because official code was unavailable. For SEMCo, we replace the
data loader and final evaluator to match our strict item-cold
splits and full-ranking item-macro protocol while keeping its

RQ3: Course-Knowledge Ablation
Core ablations on MOOCCube use the same split, candidate set, K values, and item-macro aggregation as the
main comparison. Removing educational rewards reduces
R@10/N@10 by 0.0072/0.0044. Removing the knowledgeguided sampler reduces them by 0.0053/0.0043, and removing the prerequisite auxiliary loss gives the largest earlyrank NDCG drop, decreasing N@5/N@10 by 0.0061/0.0070.
When all course-knowledge inputs are removed, scores drop
below Full CKG-RL on all six metrics. This pattern points
to course knowledge coupled with rewards, candidate construction, and auxiliary supervision rather than a gain from
one scalar signal.

RQ4: Sensitivity and Cost
The MOOCCube sweeps vary knowledge-sampling weight
β, reward-shaping scale, and simulator horizon T around the
default (β = 0.20, reward scale = 1.00, T = 5), which gives
validation R@10 = 0.2942 and N@10 = 0.2175. Across
β, validation R@10 spans 0.2831–0.2974 and N@10 spans
0.2117–0.2194. Reward scale stays narrow around 1.0, with
R@10/N@10 ranging over 0.2864–0.2944/0.2126–0.2182.
Horizons T = 1, 3, 5, 7, 10 also remain close, with T = 7
giving the lowest validation N@10 at 0.2105. These validation sweeps determine the fixed default setting; held-out test
scores in Table ?? are then reported without further hyperparameter selection.
CKG-RL averages 133.8 ± 51.8, 312.2 ± 67.4, and
279.2 ± 17.7 seconds per epoch on MOOCCube, Junyi, and
COCO. Eval-only final cold+hot inference takes 18.0 ± 0.7,

56.4 ± 0.3, and 9.8 ± 0.1 seconds, respectively. The MOOCCube profile uses the main split with a retained checkpoint
that has the same CKG-RL inference graph. The simulator
mainly affects training; cached inference remains O(QId).
The supplement adds CGRC cost-reference timing and a
cost/gain table.

Discussion and Limitations
The experiments point to the evaluation unit as a central
source of risk. Interaction-weighted metrics can improve
when a model serves a few high-volume cold courses, even if
many other cold courses receive no useful rank; distributionaware evaluation is designed to expose this pattern (??). Itemmacro full ranking makes that failure mode visible. Course
knowledge addresses a different part of the problem. Concept
overlap supplies a semantic bridge, whereas prerequisite and
difficulty signals add readiness-oriented structure.
This study still abstracts away from deployment. The
benchmarks isolate about 68 MOOCCube cold courses, 71
Junyi cold items, and 820 COCO cold items per seed, but
they remain smaller than industrial catalogs. Course relations and embeddings are dataset-specific; prerequisite and
difficulty signals approximate readiness rather than mastery.
Offline ranking does not measure learning outcomes or longterm impact, so deployment requires online and pedagogical
validation (??).

Conclusion
We studied strict item-cold MOOC recommendation, where
target courses have no training interactions and full-ranking
item-macro evaluation exposes failures hidden by interaction
averages. CKG-RL combines content anchoring, forced-cold
masking, retrieval-based simulation, and course-knowledge
rewards, improving early-rank item-cold metrics on MOOCCube, Junyi, and COCO. Under this protocol, reducing itemcold exposure risk requires both content transfer and educational constraints.

Ethical Statement
Experiments use public MOOCCube/MOOCCubeX, Junyi,
and COCO data plus derived item representations. Released
artifacts should exclude personally identifiable information
and follow dataset terms. Deployment requires online validation and monitoring for visibility, fairness, transparency,
and consequential use (??).
